# A curated website grounded in the biography

Prepared: 8 October 2026. Planning only; no website implementation in this pass.

## Direction

Make the site a welcoming introduction to a person and his work. Amudhan Adigal's biography supplies the evidence and editorial choices; the website selects the moments that help a visitor understand Thani Nayagam and choose something to read.

The central story is **a life connecting Tamil to the world**: learning Tamil deeply, bringing scholarship into conversation across languages, and creating opportunities for other people to study it.

Keep the selected Living Archive 02 palette, portrait, and spacious typography. The improvement is in the sequence, copy, and choice of material. This plan supersedes older content/navigation proposals where they conflict with the current selected design or the new biography audit.

## What a visitor should leave with

1. An understanding of who he was and why his work mattered.
2. One memorable human detail, rather than a list of credentials.
3. A clear next reading that fits their interest and language.
4. A way to inspect the evidence when they want more detail.

The initial experience serves an interested general reader. Researchers can reach the catalogue and sources directly through the same navigation.

## Three levels of detail

| Level | Where | What appears |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Introduce | Home | A short identity statement, three ideas, three selected readings, and one invitation to meet the person. |
| Understand | Life and Contributions | A short narrative and a few explained examples, supported by discreet page citations. |
| Study | Archive and document readers | Search, categories, edition metadata, available texts, source notes, and deeper chronology. |

These are levels within the existing pages, not three new sets of pages. The complete biography remains the source behind the editing. Its chapter list and appendices do not become the website's menu.

## Page responsibilities

| Existing page | Reader's question | Planned content | Default copy budget, English |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Home | Who is he, and where should I begin? | Four compact sections described below. | About 180–250 words, excluding navigation and citations. |
| Life (`/about/`) | How did this work become his life's purpose? | Four narrative acts, one human detail, compact key facts, optional chronology. | About 450–600 words in the main narrative. |
| Contributions (`/contributions/`) | What changed because of his work? | Three stories, each explaining a problem, his contribution, and a useful reading. | About 300–420 words total. |
| Archive (`/archive/`) | How do I find a particular work? | Search, five subject filters, concise catalogue results, accurate access labels. | Short orientation; metadata rather than another biography. |
| Support (`/sponsors/`) | Who maintains this, and how can I help? | Brief acknowledgement of Amudhan's documented scholarship, archive purpose, existing contribution options. | About 120–180 words around the practical information. |

Tamil should carry the same meanings and choices in natural Tamil. These are editorial targets, not rigid Tamil word limits; check the resulting line lengths and mobile page length in each language.

Each contribution teaser gets roughly 20–30 words. Each reading gets a title, original-language label, content type, and roughly 15–25 words on what the reader will discover. Labels such as “Tamil essay collection” and “English journal essay” explain the material more usefully than record counts.

The home page no longer needs its full five-category directory, repeated catalogue filters, expandable 25-record listing, four-number metrics band, nine-stop journey, and separate closing promotion. Their useful functions belong on the pages below. The selected readings link straight to the relevant text section; the full archive stays one clear navigation step away.

## Life: four short acts

| Act | What to tell | Why include it | Biography evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Discovering a wider world | His early enthusiasm for English and his encounter with other languages and cultures in Rome; the development of his Tamil interest. | Establish curiosity and change, so the visitor meets a person rather than a finished legend. | Printed pp. 2–5; Markdown lines 302, 318, 336. |
| Tamil becomes a vocation | Teaching at Vadakangulam, sustained Tamil learning, then Annamalai study: M.A. 1947 and M.Litt. 1949. | Show the patient work that underlay his later contribution. | Printed pp. 8–11; lines 374, 378, 388, 418. |
| Scholarship becomes a shared conversation | International outreach, the English-language journal, IATR, and his first-conference leadership. Briefly connect this with his concern for language and cultural fellowship. | Explain what the institutions enabled people to do. | Printed pp. 12–23, 24–40, 65–71, 97–108; lines 455, 576, 1019, 1082, 1104, 1357–1363. |
| Continuing the work | Later teaching, return to Jaffna, continued scholarship and public lectures, and his death in 1980. | Show a life of service beyond the major institutional milestones. | Printed pp. 199–202; lines 2477, 2481, 2487, 2501, 2511, 2513. |

Use **one human detail in the main narrative**: while playing football with his students, he asked them to regard him as a companion on the field. The biography records this on p. 8, line 378. Paraphrase it in one or two sentences within the teaching act. Avoid turning the page into a collection of anecdotes.

The biography's book-exhibition humour is an optional alternative for a reading introduction: even seeing the covers had value when people had not read all the books (pp. 43–44, lines 782, 786, 790). Use it only if it improves the page; do not make an unattributed quotation or invent an English verbatim version.

A small reference panel can hold birth/death dates, vocation, and the two doctorates. Exact school lists, teachers, full language lists, appointment details, and honours are available through source notes or the optional chronology where they explain a turning point.

Place an **8–10-entry chronology** on the existing Life page behind “Explore key dates.” Each entry has one sentence explaining what changed, its year, and a source note. Separate M.A. and M.Litt., distinguish conference office from IATR office, and use the corrected Paris/Naples dates. The full research chronology stays in the audit/workbench rather than being copied into this display.

Each story gets roughly 90–130 words and at most one relevant documentary image. Describe the contribution and its effect before listing dates. Preserve collaborators and the distinction between discovery, photography, editing, and republication.

The complete publications register moves to Archive. A short reading link within each story is enough. The present four-image strip should become images attached to the stories they explain, with captions that identify the actual object and publication date.

These are proposed entry points, conditional on inspection of the actual reading text and headings. Do not promise a whole book when the available reader contains related articles, a partial transcription, or a finding aid. For example, the current conference wrapper describes proceedings information rather than full papers, and the Proença reader contains 1964 introductory articles rather than the 1966 dictionary edition.

## Archive and readers

- Archive owns the full catalogue, search, and subject filters. Give each result one main action with its correct state: read text, inspect information, or visit the source.
- Distinguish original work, represented edition, and current reading material. A first-publication year is not automatically the date of the reading copy.
- A reader begins with a short orientation: what this text is, its original language, its edition or journal issue, and why it is relevant.
- Keep contents, reading controls, and source information available without placing long preparatory essays before the actual text.
- Language switching changes the interface and contextual explanation. Show clearly when the original text remains Tamil or English.
- Support keeps its acknowledgement of Amudhan's 1993 authorship concise. Use separately documented current relationships for statements about this archive's guidance or endorsement.

## Quiet, usable evidence

Use a small source link beside the relevant paragraph or at the end of a narrative act. Its expanded note names Amudhan, the 1993 book, and printed pages. The visitor sees a readable bibliographic citation rather than an internal source ID or an audit verdict.

The internal fact ledger retains the exact Markdown lines, source conflicts, and confidence decisions. It is the editor's working record. Public explanations stay focused on the supported story; unresolved details can be omitted or explicitly attributed when they matter.

A claim should generally be explained on one page. Home offers an invitation; Life explains development; Contributions explains effects; the reader supplies the text and edition. This prevents the same dates, achievements, and catalogue lists from appearing in every section.

## Navigation and visual treatment

- Use one main row: **Life / Contributions / Documents / Support**, plus the home logo and language switch. Localise the labels in Tamil.
- Place category filters within Documents. Consolidate the present second subject-navigation row with those filters rather than repeat the same discovery choices across the site.
- Let the portrait establish identity. Use archival images where they explain a particular story; image caption and story must refer to the same object.
- Keep large, comfortable Tamil and English text, short paragraphs, generous spacing, and a consistent editorial hierarchy.
- On mobile, arrange story and reading cards in a simple vertical sequence. Optional dates and source notes expand with clear, keyboard-accessible controls.
- Keep the footer compact, with main destinations, source information, and archive/contact details.

## Editorial acceptance rules

1. Every visible historical claim has an evidence reference in the internal ledger.
2. A homepage addition must earn its place by helping someone understand him or choose a reading; otherwise it belongs deeper in the site.
3. The home stays within four sections and three featured readings.
4. The life narrative has four acts and one main anecdote; extra detail is optional.
5. Contributions explains three stories rather than presenting a complete list of achievements.
6. Counts appear only when they clarify a story and their scope is explicit. Supported prose replaces uncertain promotional numbers.
7. Tamil and English share a meaning and source basis; neither is a rough duplicate with untranslated summary fields.
8. More biography material does not automatically mean more website sections.

## Implementation — 8 October 2026

The user subsequently authorized a full redesign, clarifying that the person and his archive must remain central. The implemented hero therefore leads with Thani Nayagam Adigal’s name and portrait; thematic learning and scholarly connections serve as context for his collection.
