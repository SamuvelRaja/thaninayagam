To spite an enemy king, there was also the custom
among victors of making use of the timber of the
“* suardian trees ’’ of their enemy chiefs or kings, for
the wooden part of the drums that were used by their
armies in proclaiming their victories,’

Besides these “ guardian trees,” the three kings
of Tamil Nad, the Cera, the Cola, and the Pandiya
had a flower each as his own emblem, just as the lily,
the rose, and other flowers have been taken as emb-
lems of royal houses in the West. The aiti flower
was the emblem of the Colas, the palmyrah flower of
the Cera, and the margosa flower of the Pandya.
