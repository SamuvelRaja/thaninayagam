According to the literary legislation governing
ancient Tamil poetry, each one of the five regional
landscapes formed the background of poems dealing
with definite groups of subjects of love and warfare.
In agam poetry, a poet wishing to write a poem on
love regarding the union of lovers, their first meetings,
their nocturnal trysts, their conversation among them-
selves or with the maid, the companion of the he-
toine, had to choose the mountain scenery as the
background of his poems. Having located the scene of
his poem in the mountains, he was not free to choose
his setting in any season of the year (Qu@tb Gumgpsi),
Of the six seasons of the Tamil year he had to choose
the season of the coldest part of the year (s=\*, the
cold season), the most rainy, and the most beset with
difficulties. These two were the most characteristic
months of the mountain regions when mountainous
climate is most itself, namely, during the two lunar
months October and November, corresponding ap-
proximately to mid-October to mid-December of the
solar year. Neither was he free to describe his sub-
ject or his characters with any of the six divisions
of the day of twenty-four hours (Sg! @umgps!) chosen
as the scene of his poetic setting. He had preferably
