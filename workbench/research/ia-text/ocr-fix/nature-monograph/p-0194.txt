tions of Nature’s scenes in the Aeneid, Vergil has a
few passages where he shows that early in the life
of Europe he apprehended a sense of sympathy
between man and Nature. In the sixth book, the
earth trembles and the mountains quake as if in
dread of the approach of the Sibyl, and in the fourth
book there is maintained throughout “a fine sym-
