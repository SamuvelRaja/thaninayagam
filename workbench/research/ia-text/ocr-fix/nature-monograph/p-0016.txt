. An abundance of similes and metaphors regard-
ing Nature and exquisite touches of suggestion are
to be found in the Purananuru and Patirrupaitu.
Some of the poems in the former collection may be
easily classed among the best of Nature poetry in
world literature. But the poetry of the Agam col-
lections introduces, in addition, an outlook that is
foreign to every other literature. It has been the object
of writers like Humboldt, Ruskin, Biese and Palgrave
to regard the ancient and modern world, when looked
at from the standpoint of the poetic interpretation
of Nature as “one great confederation.” Though
the study of ancient Tamil literature confirms this
view in a general way, it also shows that in a corner of
peninsular India a people developed an interpreta-
tion of Nature the like of which was never conceived
on the plains watered by the Ganges, or on the banks
= the Nile, or the Tiber, or on the shores of the Aegean
