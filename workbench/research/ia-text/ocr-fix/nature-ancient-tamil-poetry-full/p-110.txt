The trees, or rather the gods that were supposed
to dwell within them, were worshipped according to
customary ritual. Offerings of balls of rice and
flesh were made on the altar that would be sometimes
erected at the foot of the tree. The crows would
preferably congregate their nests in the vicinity
and feed themselves on these offerings, and at
night the hoot of the owls provoked fear in the hearts
of the neighbourhood.”

The maram tree or sea-side Indian oak was said
to be the habitat of a deity adorned with great awe
and reverence, because it was believed to chastise
evil-doers. Probably it was this idea of chastising
the wicked that made the ancients plant the memorial
stones of heroes fallen in battle or frontier fray pre-
ferably beneath the shade of these trees.° The
god would continue to chastise their enemies as their
heroes had done while alive. That these gods were
not merely terrifying deities, but were also believed
