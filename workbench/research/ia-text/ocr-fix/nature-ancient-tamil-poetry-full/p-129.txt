“ Like linked gems are nocei’s curling ringlets blue

Mid all the flowering trees is none whose tender hue

So fills the soul with love as thine whose blooming wreath

Since cities were mostly situated in the riverine plains,

setting out to besiege the ramparts, say the commen-
tators, it fittingly corresponded to its agam couuter-

There are two other divisions of agam poctry which
have no landseape background prescribed in Tol-
kappiyar, probably because these were an addition
made after the Tamils had codified the five-fold
division. These are outside the course of mutual
love, Peruntinai or lust, and Kaikilai or unrequited
love. These may occur with any of the aforesaid
landscapes as background, but since they do not
enter directly into our theme, it is sufficient to have
made mention of them. Their puram counterparts
ave kanji and padan, respectively, the former comp-
rising all poems on the transitoriness of the world,
and the latter all eulogistie and panegyric verse,
