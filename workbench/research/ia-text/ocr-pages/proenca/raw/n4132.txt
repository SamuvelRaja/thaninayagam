84. tale “Pano, que dad os maridos as
molheres quando the amarrad o tale,
item absolute pano.” (Cloth, which
husbands give wives when they fasten
the talee on them, also absolutely cloth.)

& mp (848)

See Dalgado, II, 348, tale, derived by
him from Tamil-Malayalam /dli. See
HJ1, 678: HJ2, 891-899, s.v. “ talee,”
defined as “A small trinket of gold
which is fastened by a string round the
neck of a married woman in S. India.
Possibly connected with Arabic tahlil...
the origin of Spanish fali, a “ baldrick "'
or “bride's betrothal band.” See Webs-
ter's II, 2572, “tali," with etymology
given as Kanarese and Tamil /dli. Per-
haps there should be mentioned also
Malay iali, “rope, cord, string, line.”
It would not be impossible for the
trinket to be confused with the string
to which it is attached.
