142 TAMIL OULTURE

94. Canaaor “Huma casta de figos compridos,
como os de canaaor.” (A type of long
figs, like those of Canara.)

Qurrgeruuypw (153)
This probably is the piace name
“Canara”, (HJ1, 117-118; HJ2, 152-
154), properly kannada, “ black country,”
that is, the Dravidian kar, “black,”
and nddu, “ country.”

25. Carambula “Huma fruita, pera caril na figura,
como carabu-la gonsali.” (a fruit, curry
pear (?) in shape, like the carambola
gonsali.)

Smswarw (260)
See HJ1, 122-123; HJ2, 160, “caram-
bola,” the fruit name derived perhaps
from Marathi karanbal, or karambela
(ultimately from Sanskrit karmara). See
also Webster's 401, s.v. “ carambola.”

26. Caranes “'H. ou m. que mostrad caranes o
rosto oarregado.”’ (Man or:woman that
shows a forbidding countenance. Cf.
Portuguese cardo, ‘large, ugly face.’’)

Qury (153)

H. and m. are abbreviations of Homem
and molher. This word seems to be
identical with the final part of the
compound mott-k-kdran (Lexicon, VI,
3884), an expression meaning “man
with forbidding countenance, stylish
man.” There may be a connection with
karana, “the name of one of the
(so-called) mixt castes of the Hindus,
sprung from a Sudra mother and
Vaisya father". See HJ2, 273-274, s.v.
“cranny ”.
