## 1. Subject and content

**English name:** Rev. Dr. Xavier S. Thani Nayagam Adigal.

**Tamil name:** அருள்திரு. முனைவர் சேவியர் தனிநாயகம் அடிகள்.

**Archive name:** Thani Nayagam Digital Archive / தனிநாயக அடிகள் ஆவணகம்.

**Life dates:** 1913–1980; born 2 August 1913 at Karampon, Jaffna; died 1 September 1980.

He was a Catholic priest, polyglot scholar, teacher, editor, and international advocate for Tamil studies. His work included Tamil literature, culture, comparative thought, education, research publishing, and scholarly collaboration across countries.

The existing editorial framing describes a life connecting Tamil to the world. This is content context, not a required visual metaphor or headline.

### Owner-selected visual reference

The owner's description:

> “these are the design reference brush stroke caligraphy style titles typography for heading and red sun brush strokes round background for images”

The references establish two visual treatments:

- **Heading typography:** brush-stroke calligraphy with painted letterforms, varied stroke weight, uneven edges, and visible brush texture. This applies to titles and headings; body typography has not been specified.
- **Image backdrop:** a red sun-like circular form made from brush strokes, with irregular edges and visible gaps or sweeps. The subject image sits in front of this painted circular background.

Additional observations from the supplied images include white or pale space, black painted lettering, red focal areas, monochrome figures, and broad black brush marks. These are observations, not additional requirements automatically imposed on the archive.

The reference images supply visual treatment. Their poster wording, logos, credits, people, and depicted scenes are not archive content. The historical subject, text, and supplied documentary assets remain the content context.

## 8. Facts relevant to visible text and captions

The main biographical source is Amudhan Adigal's 1993 biography, **தவத்திரு தனிநாயகம் அடிகளார்**, International Institute of Tamil Studies.

- The life dates are 1913–1980.
- M.A.: 1947. M.Litt.: 1949.
- Tamil Culture began in 1952; the 1952–1966 span includes publication gaps, with twelve publication years described in the biography.
- The 200-plus lecture count concerns one year during his United States tour, not a worldwide count of university-only lectures.
- Nine languages are explicitly described as deeply studied; an unqualified “12+ languages mastered” statement is not supported by this source.
- Four early world Tamil conferences are part of the content, but his role differed between them. He was deputy chairman with scholarly leadership at the first Kuala Lumpur conference.
- IATR was established in 1964.
- Paris teaching is recorded at Collège de France in 1970; Naples in 1971.
- Early-print research involved collaborators and distinct activities such as locating, documenting, editing, translating, and republishing.

Numerical content includes 9 languages deeply studied, 200+ talks in one US-tour year, four conferences with differing roles, and twelve publication years. These are facts with qualifications, not a recommendation for any visual treatment.

Publication dates, represented editions, reprint dates, and image provenance should not be conflated. Exact historical quotations require a source; the current hero introductions are editorial prose. English presentation does not mean a Tamil source text has an English translation.
